What’s coming

Last updated 2 October 2026

SendOff is built while it is being used, and this is where it is heading. Nothing here has a date on it: a race weekend decides more of the order than a plan does. Have an idea or a wish? Use Send feedback in the menu, or write to info@sendoff.run.

Next up in this order

  1. Time-based races. 24-hour obstacle races, backyard ultras and 6, 12 or 24-hour loops, where the clock is fixed and the laps are the result. The pit board works as it does now; the race page counts laps, shows the time left (or, for a backyard, the time to the next bell), and knows when a racer is done.
  2. Cheers and messages. People following at home can send a one-tap cheer or a short message. It waits for the crew at the next aid station, who read it out, so the racer hears it in person and their phone never buzzes on a climb.
  3. Goal-time planner. Put in the finish time you are aiming for and get a target arrival time at every aid station, worked out from the distance and the climbing of each leg. On race day the pit board and the racer screen show how far ahead of or behind the plan you are at each checkpoint.
  4. Printable crew sheet. One page per crew member to print the night before: the checkpoints in order, the cutoffs as clock times, the target times, and room to write. Paper has no battery, and it can be handed to a pacer who just showed up.
  5. Download your data. Every race you have logged as a spreadsheet (CSV), as JSON, and the course as GPX, so a season of racing is always yours to take with you.

Shipped lately

  • Public racer profiles. Switch it on and your profile becomes a page anyone can open at sendoff.run/@ and your username: your races, results and personal bests. It is off unless you switch it on, private races never appear on it, and your race reports and fueling numbers show only on the races where you choose.
  • Usernames. Pick a handle like @jason on your profile page, so people can find you and add you to a race without needing your email address. It is not used to sign in, you choose whether people can find you by it, and it is the start of following a racer.
  • Your racer profile. Every race you have run in one place, with totals, personal bests kept apart by discipline, and room after each race for your official time, your place, a link to the results and a few lines on how it went. Races from before SendOff can be added by hand, and a long list can be searched and filtered.
  • Sign in with Google, for an account you already have, and an Account box in the menu to see how you sign in and connect or disconnect Google.
  • Race photos, live. Crew and racers post photos from the course, filed under the leg they were taken on, and everyone following sees them within seconds. There is a camera button for when there is no signal to browse a gallery.
  • Racing without a crew. The racer screen works on its own for a racer with nobody at the aid stations: one tap to log a gel, a flask or a medicine on the move, a tap to take a mistake back, and the items in whatever order you put them.
  • One-tap items for the things handed over by the piece, including ones with no numbers to count, like a medicine. Before the start, the pit board and racer screen show them as a preview, so nobody wonders where logging went.
  • Private and unlisted races. A private race only opens for the people on it. A public race can be taken off the hub and put back.
  • A new course for an existing race, for when the route changes after the race is set up.
  • Obstacle course racing as its own discipline, with its own personal bests.

Being considered no order, no promises

  • Finding a race among many on the hub: search and filters by distance, discipline and date.
  • Weather at each aid station for the time the racer is expected there.
  • Follow a racer or a race by their username, and be told when they start and when they reach a checkpoint.
  • Start from last year’s race: the course, the aid stations and the crew carried over to a new date.
  • Sign in with Facebook, which is built and waiting on approval from Meta.

What stays true

Following a race needs no account and no app. Every public race stays readable for good. What SendOff keeps about you, and who can see it, is in the privacy policy.
