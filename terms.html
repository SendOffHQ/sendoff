1. What SendOff does

SendOff lets a crew record a racer's progress through a race: arrivals and departures at aid stations, food and fluid, notes. It shows that to whoever has the link. It works out simple arithmetic from what has been logged, including pace, projected finish, and margin against a cutoff.

2. It is in beta

3. It is not a safety system, and it is not official timing

SendOff is a convenience for a crew. It is not:

  • the race's official timing. The race organisation's clock is the one that counts.
  • a tracking or location system. It knows where a racer is only because somebody typed it in.
  • a safety or emergency system. It cannot tell that a racer is in trouble, and it will not tell anybody if they are.

Do not use SendOff to decide whether somebody is safe, whether they should continue, or whether they have made a cutoff. Those decisions belong to the racer, their crew and the race organisation, using the race's own information.

Everything SendOff shows is arithmetic on what was typed in. If a split was logged late, or the phone had no signal, or somebody pressed the wrong button, the numbers will be wrong, and they will not look wrong.

4. Every public race stays readable

A public race stays readable by anybody, with no account and no app, for as long as SendOff runs. That includes after it has finished: the splits, the course, the charts and the printout are all still there. There is no limit on how many of your past races you can see, and there will not be one.
