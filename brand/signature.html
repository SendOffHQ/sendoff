If the button cannot reach your clipboard, select the signature above with the mouse, from the logo to the last link, and copy it by hand. The result is the same.

There is no name on it, because info@ is the company and not a person. To sign as yourself, paste it into Gmail and type your name on the line above the tagline, in bold. The logo already says SendOff, so a line of text repeating it is the one thing worth leaving off.

Putting it in Gmail

  1. Copy the signature, above.
  2. Gmail → the gear icon → See all settings → General, and scroll to Signature.
  3. Create new, and name it SendOff. The name is only ever seen by you.
  4. Click into the box and paste. Do not retype anything: pasting is what carries the logo and the colours.
  5. Under Signature defaults, change the address dropdown from your own address to info@sendoff.run, then set FOR NEW EMAILS USE to SendOff. That dropdown is the whole trick: signatures are per sending address, and setting one while your personal address is selected puts it on your personal mail instead.
  6. Repeat that last step for invites@sendoff.run if you send as that too.
  7. Save Changes, at the very bottom of the page.

On replies, consider leaving it as No signature. A thread that repeats the logo on every message reads as a newsletter rather than as a person answering.

Before you trust it

Send one to yourself and open it on a phone. Two things to look for: the logo loading at all, since many clients block images until asked, and the whole thing sitting inside the width of the screen. The alt text is styled to fall back to the word SendOff in the right weight, so a blocked image costs the picture and not the name.
